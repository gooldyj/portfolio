Comprehensive mini-course development for Dodge City Community College service learning certification

STAKEHOLDERS

Dodge City Community College Students, Faculty, and Administration

METHODOLOGY

Evidence-based ADDIE instructional design framework

EXPECTED OUTCOMES

Enhanced student engagement and community impact measurement

PROJECT OVERVIEW

The mini-course is structured to provide participants with a clear understanding of what service learning is, how it differs from community service, and how it can be integrated into academic courses to enhance student engagement and civic responsibility. Course components include:

  • Foundational Modules: An introduction to service learning theory, benefits, and alignment with institutional learning outcomes.
  • Practical Applications: Case studies drawn from DC3 service-learning initiatives such as food security projects, animal welfare partnerships, and civic advocacy campaigns.
  • Interactive Elements: Multimedia lessons, scenario-based activities, and reflective prompts designed to promote active learning and practical skill-building.
  • Assessment and Reflection: Embedded quizzes and reflection assignments that measure comprehension and encourage application of service learning concepts in real instructional contexts.

This project integrates technology-enhanced learning strategies, including multimedia creation, AI-assisted scripting, and digital delivery platforms, to model best practices in instructional design. It also reflects my commitment to inclusive education by ensuring that content is accessible, adaptable, and relevant to diverse learners.

The Service Learning Mini-Course represents a sustainable resource for DC3, equipping faculty and students with the tools to connect academic learning to community impact. It embodies my broader vision of education as a transformative process that prepares learners to think critically, act ethically, and engage meaningfully with the world around them.

METHODOLOGY

The design and development of the Service Learning Mini-Course followed an instructional design methodology grounded in the ADDIE framework:

1. Analysis

  • Identified the need for a standardized, accessible introduction to service learning at Dodge City Community College.
  • Conducted a review of institutional goals, Common Learning Outcomes, and stakeholder expectations to ensure alignment.
  • Analyzed learner characteristics, including faculty, staff, and students with varying levels of familiarity with service learning.

2. Design

  • Established learning objectives focused on defining service learning, differentiating it from community service, and demonstrating its application in academic contexts.
  • Selected digital delivery methods to maximize accessibility and scalability.
  • Designed multimedia components, quizzes, and reflection activities to promote engagement and measure outcomes.

3. Development

  • Created instructional materials including video modules (scripted with AI assistance), interactive activities, and reflective prompts.
  • Incorporated examples from DC3 service-learning initiatives (e.g., food security drives, animal welfare partnerships, microchipping ordinance advocacy).
  • Ensured accessibility through clear formatting, closed captioning, and multiple means of content representation.

4. Implementation

  • Deployed the mini-course in a digital format suitable for integration into Canvas or standalone use.
  • Facilitated initial pilot with select faculty and student participants for feedback and validation.

5. Evaluation

  • Built in formative assessments (quizzes, reflections) to measure learner comprehension.
  • Collected feedback from pilot participants to refine course clarity, relevance, and accessibility.
  • Established plans for summative evaluation based on learner outcomes and faculty adoption in future semesters.

EXPECTED OUTCOMES

The Service Learning Mini-Course is designed to achieve the following outcomes:

1. Knowledge and Awareness

ANALYSIS

NEEDS ASSESSMENT & RESEARCH

PROJECT CONTEXT & PROBLEM STATEMENT

The Service Learning Program at Dodge City Community College gives students the chance to apply classroom knowledge in real community settings, creating experiences that are both academic and civic. While the program has been successful in connecting students with partners across the community, one major challenge has been the uneven way students are prepared to participate.

At present, each instructor introduces service learning in their own way, which means that some students arrive at their placements with a solid understanding of what is expected, while others begin with very little orientation. Faculty must also spend valuable class time covering the same ground each semester.

What is missing is a consistent, college-wide training that introduces students to the program, clarifies expectations, and sets a shared standard of readiness before they begin their work in the field. This training will be developed as a stand-alone mini-course that all students complete in advance. It can also be embedded directly into Canvas if instructors prefer to integrate it.

TARGET AUDIENCE

The training is designed for undergraduate students enrolled in courses with a service learning component. Most are traditional students between the ages of eighteen and twenty-four, though adult learners also participate.

While many are familiar with a learning management system, their levels of digital literacy vary. The course must therefore be intuitive and accessible, with captions, transcripts, and mobile-friendly access.

Motivation also varies widely. Some students approach service learning with enthusiasm for civic engagement, while others participate mainly to meet course requirements. The training must connect program expectations to personal goals and prior experiences, fostering engagement across a wide range of learners.

TRAINING STRATEGY (ADDIE)

The ADDIE model—Analysis, Design, Development, Implementation, and Evaluation—will guide this project. ADDIE is appropriate because it provides a step-by-step, systematic process to ensure alignment between learner needs, content design, and outcomes.

Analysis

  • Clarify expectations with the client, gather input from faculty, and identify recurring challenges in preparation.
  • Account for student characteristics such as schedules, technology access, and prior knowledge.

Design

  • Translate findings into learning objectives, storyboard the content flow, and align quiz items with objectives.
  • Accessibility features will be built in from the start.

Development

  • Build the mini-course using any appropriate technology—screen captures, motion graphics, templated animations, licensed media, and on-screen text.
  • Create captions and transcripts.
  • Integrate quiz items into the content flow and configure a completion certificate that issues automatically once requirements are met.
  • Test the course on multiple devices to ensure accessibility and performance.

Implementation

  • Pilot the course with a small group of students and instructors.
  • Gather feedback on clarity, navigation, and usefulness.
  • Provide faculty with a simple guide to verify student completion.
  • Adjust as needed before campus-wide rollout.

Evaluation

  • Keep the evaluation practical.
  • Consult directly with a few students and faculty after the pilot for feedback.
  • Continue occasional check-ins once in use to confirm students arrive better prepared.

THEORETICAL FOUNDATION

Constructivist learning theory supports this approach because students learn best when connecting new knowledge to their prior experiences and applying it in authentic contexts. By including scenarios, reflection prompts, and clear expectations, the training encourages learners to construct meaning that transfers to their service experiences.

INSTRUCTIONAL TECHNOLOGY

The training will operate as a stand-alone program that students can complete independently. To maximize flexibility, it can also be embedded into Canvas if faculty wish to integrate it into their courses.

Quiz items and certificates will be managed in the learning management system, while the program itself will remain self-contained for repeated use. This dual structure offers both flexibility and scalability.

PROJECT TIMELINE

  • October 2025: Development (build video modules with available technology, author quizzes, create transcripts and captions).
  • November 2025: Implementation pilot (test with a small group of students and faculty, collect feedback, revise).
  • December 5, 2025: Final delivery and presentation to the client.

FUNDING & STAFFING

The training will be created using existing institutional resources, including media tools and the learning management system. No external funding is required. Oversight will be provided by the Vice President of Academics, the client for this project.

CONCLUSION

By applying the ADDIE model and grounding the design in constructivist principles, this project will result in a concise, accessible mini-course that prepares all DC3 service learning students to begin their placements with confidence.

Its stand-alone structure ensures consistency, while its ability to embed into Canvas provides flexibility for faculty. The embedded quiz and certificate guarantee accountability while the design respects the realities of student time, faculty workloads, and community partner expectations.

DESIGN & DEV

INSTRUCTIONAL ARCHITECTURE

PROJECT OVERVIEW

The Service Learning Program at Dodge City Community College provides students with opportunities to extend classroom learning into the community through structured service experiences. Although the program has been successful in fostering engagement, preparation varies because each instructor introduces the orientation differently. Some students begin placements with clear expectations, while others lack a common baseline.

This report presents the design and development plan for a stand-alone orientation mini-course—delivered as short video modules with embedded quizzes and a completion certificate—that students complete prior to placement. The mini-course functions independently and can also be embedded in Canvas for access and tracking.

This project follows the ADDIE instructional design approach, which provides a flexible structure for analysis, design, development, implementation, and evaluation. Each step guides decisions and ensures alignment between objectives, materials, and evaluation.

LEARNING OBJECTIVES

First-Level Objectives:

  1. Demonstrate readiness to participate successfully in the DC3 Service Learning Program.
  2. Demonstrate knowledge of program expectations through successful completion of a standardized training module.

Second-Level Objectives:

  • 1.1 Explain the purpose of the Service Learning Program and its connection to academic learning and community needs. (Bloom's: Understand)
  • 1.2 Identify student responsibilities (attendance, communication, reflection). (Bloom's: Remember/Understand)
  • 1.3 Apply program knowledge by responding correctly to embedded quiz items with at least 80% accuracy. (Bloom's: Apply)
  • 2.1 Earn a certificate of completion within the H5P module after meeting performance requirements. (Bloom's: Apply)

These objectives were developed using the ABCD approach (Kurt, 2019).

CONTENT & INSTRUCTIONAL MATERIALS

The mini-course contains three focused modules:

  1. Program Overview
  2. Student Responsibilities
  3. Reflection & Next Steps

Instructional materials include:

  • Synthesia-generated video narration refined with Adobe Creative Cloud
  • Embedded H5P quizzes that provide immediate feedback
  • Captions and transcripts for accessibility
  • An H5P add-on that issues a completion certificate after the learner meets the 80% threshold across items

The instructional prototype can be found in Appendix A.

JUSTIFICATION OF INSTRUCTIONAL METHOD

A self-paced mini-course ensures every student receives the same orientation without consuming repeated class time. Short video segments reduce cognitive load and allow learners to review as needed. Embedded quizzes promote active processing and confirm understanding before students begin placements, which is especially important for learners managing work and family responsibilities alongside coursework.

This strategy reflects constructivist learning theory, which emphasizes that learners build understanding through active engagement. By requiring learners to respond to embedded quiz items and reflect on their responsibilities, the mini-course promotes active construction of knowledge rather than passive reception.

JUSTIFICATION OF TRAINING SOLUTION & MEDIA

Synthesia enables efficient creation of professional narration, while Adobe tools provide editing and design polish for clarity and consistency. H5P supports interactive questions and certificate issuance through an add-on, creating a complete, self-contained learning experience that embeds cleanly in Canvas for access and tracking.

This combination delivers quality, accountability, and sustainability at low cost. This process aligns with rapid instructional design principles (Piskurich, 2015).

PILOT STUDY PLAN

A pilot will run in November 2025 with 10–15 students and 2–3 faculty. The pilot will confirm clarity, navigation, quiz accuracy, and certificate generation.

Feedback will be collected through a short form and brief faculty consultations, and completion data will be reviewed. Revisions will be completed prior to full deployment in December 2025.

EVALUATION PLAN

Evaluation remains practical and actionable.

Formative Evaluation:

  • Faculty review of draft materials
  • November pilot testing

Summative Evaluation:

  • Quiz scores (≥80%)
  • Certificate completion logs
  • Brief reflection prompts embedded in the module
  • Faculty debriefs on student preparedness

These measures provide objective evidence of mastery and qualitative insight without imposing unnecessary burden.

CONCLUSION

This design and development plan establishes the objectives, content, delivery approach, and evaluation strategy for a concise, accessible orientation to DC3 Service Learning.

By combining Synthesia and Adobe for video production with H5P for interactive quizzes and certificate issuance, the mini-course provides a consistent and flexible solution that can be embedded in Canvas. A November pilot will inform final adjustments prior to December deployment.

EVALUATION

OUTCOMES & ASSESSMENT

PROJECT OVERVIEW

This instructional design project addressed the need for a structured and accessible Service-Learning Mini Course intended for college students preparing to participate in service-learning experiences. Prior to this project, students received service-learning expectations inconsistently through informal conversations or brief in-class announcements, which led to uncertainty, uneven levels of understanding, and students entering community partnerships without adequate preparation.

The purpose of the project was to design a standardized onboarding course that students would complete before beginning their service-learning work. The completed instructional product consists of instructional video modules, embedded knowledge checks, and an automated certificate confirming completion.

The original implementation plan included embedding quizzes directly into instructional video content with automatic certificate generation. However, during pilot testing the certificate feature repeatedly failed, requiring redesign. The revised implementation uses an independently hosted instructional website that houses the video instruction, quiz, and automated certificate tools and functions reliably across devices.

TARGET LEARNERS & REQUIREMENTS

The target learners for this training are college students enrolled in courses that include a service-learning requirement. Learners represent diverse levels of academic preparation, technological comfort, and experience with community engagement. Most students have limited professional experience and therefore require explicit expectations and structured preparation.

Learners show strong motivation when training connects to real community impact and tangible outcomes, such as receiving a certificate verifying preparedness.

To be adequately prepared for participation in service learning, students must:

  • Define service learning and distinguish it from volunteerism
  • Understand expectations for professional and ethical conduct
  • Learn required safety and communication procedures
  • Recognize the roles and responsibilities of community partners
  • Demonstrate comprehension through a scored quiz
  • Present proof of completion before entering community settings

The design context required that the mini course function across face-to-face, hybrid, and online environments and support independent completion outside of class time. The training system also needed to be compatible with laptops, Chromebooks, tablets, and phones and scalable for future departmental expansion.

INSTRUCTIONAL DESIGN APPROACH

The ADDIE framework guided every stage of the project. Instructional strategies included:

  • Short video-based learning segments for improved focus
  • Embedded formative assessments to support comprehension
  • Examples drawn from real DC3 service-learning partnerships to strengthen authenticity

Motivational elements aligned with the ARCS model emphasized relevance, confidence, engagement, and satisfaction through certification.

Initially, the project relied on tools capable of embedding assessments directly into the video and automatically generating certificates. When pilot testing revealed repeated certificate-generation failure, the design shifted to an independently hosted instructional website to ensure reliability and provide a more polished and intuitive navigation structure.

EVALUATION FEEDBACK & REVISIONS

Evaluation feedback indicated three priority improvement needs:

  1. Dependable automated certificate generation
  2. Simplified linear navigation

Revision actions included:

  • Building a standalone instructional website to house the complete course
  • Rebuilding the quiz and certificate systems using web-based scripting
  • Simplifying navigation to improve flow
  • Redesigning the visual layout

Internal testing confirmed that certificate generation performed reliably across devices and browsers.

CONCLUSION & LESSONS LEARNED

Completing the Service-Learning Mini Course using the ADDIE model reinforced the importance of iterative refinement, feedback-driven revision, and flexibility in instructional design. Although the initial certificate functionality failed, the redesign produced a stronger, more reliable, and scalable final product.

The finished course provides consistent onboarding for students participating in service learning, clearly communicates expectations, and verifies readiness through certificate documentation.

The experience demonstrated that instructional design is cyclical and strengthened through evaluation, testing, and improvement. The final result is more effective because of the challenges encountered during development.

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

PROJECT PURPOSE

The Service-Learning Mini-Course and Interactive Website Project was designed, developed, implemented, and evaluated to strengthen student engagement and community partnership opportunities at Dodge City Community College. The project addressed the need for a structured and accessible platform for students participating in service-learning courses to receive consistent training, orientation, and support.

Prior to this project, service-learning preparation varied by instructor and course type, resulting in an inconsistent understanding of core expectations, reflection strategies, and community relationship standards. This mini-course provided a unified instructional system that ensured students had the knowledge and tools necessary to succeed in authentic community-based work.

COMPLETED PROJECT DELIVERABLES

The completed project included the development of a digital orientation learning experience hosted on an interactive website. The course guided students through:

  • The purpose and value of service-learning
  • Expectations for professionalism and communication
  • Reflection practices
  • Project planning steps
  • Safety and ethical considerations

Instructional modules included video-based instruction, multimedia content, embedded formative assessments, and an automatically generated completion certificate. The website was built to function as a centralized information hub that could be used by any academic discipline at DC3 offering service-learning, rather than being limited to a single program such as Composition I.

INSTRUCTIONAL DESIGN PROCESS

The instructional design process followed the principles of learner-centered design and the ADDIE framework. During the analysis phase, the project focused on identifying systemic needs related to consistency, clarity, and scalability across service-learning experiences.

Existing practices showed that expectations, communication norms, and preparatory materials were not always aligned across courses or departments. Based on this analysis, learning objectives were developed to support students' understanding of the purpose of service-learning, project planning, community partnership etiquette, and reflection as a tool for personal and academic growth.

DEVELOPMENT & MATERIALS

Development of instructional materials was grounded in multimedia design and accessibility principles. The course incorporated narration, visuals, examples drawn from previous service-learning experiences, and structured learning activities.

Formative assessment was integrated into each module to allow students to check understanding and receive feedback. The final module contained a summative knowledge check and certificate generator, allowing students to provide proof of completion to their instructors and community partners.

IMPLEMENTATION & PILOT TESTING

Implementation was supported through a pilot test that involved a focus group consisting of four student participants and three faculty/administrative reviewers.

Feedback gathered during this pilot indicated that the lessons were clear, engaging, and useful in preparing students for real-world service work. The review process identified a technical issue related to certificate printing, which was resolved following testing. The final version operated as intended and successfully generated completion certificates without errors.

EVALUATION & OUTCOMES

Evaluation showed that the project met its intended goals. Participants reported increased clarity and confidence regarding service-learning expectations and professional collaboration with community partners.

Faculty reviewers noted that the mini-course provided a more consistent learning foundation across service-learning courses and streamlined preparatory communication.

The project also supported discussions about expanding service-learning opportunities and strengthening partnerships with community organizations such as the city attorney's office, local shelters, civic groups, and nonprofit agencies. The website now functioned as a scalable solution that could grow with future initiatives and support continued improvement of service-learning engagement across campus.

CONCLUSION

The Service-Learning Mini-Course and Interactive Website provided Dodge City Community College with an effective and sustainable instructional resource that unified expectations, improved student preparation, and reinforced the academic and civic purpose of service-learning.

The project successfully met its objectives and contributed meaningful value to both the institution and its community partners.
